# Clank-lit

**An interactive piece about how AI coding assistants give you false confidence — and quietly micro-gaslight you.**

▶️ **[Play it live](https://saintpepsi.github.io/clank-lit/)**

---

## What is this?

You open "Clank-lit.io", an AI agent that builds software by chatting. You ask for a wellness app (or a marketplace, or a multiplayer game). It enthusiastically agrees — then *gently* talks you down into "just a simple todo app to start," promising you'll grow it into the real thing later.

Then you build. You ask for features; it adds them, each time beaming **"All 52 tests passing ✅."** A working todo app becomes a Jira-grade pile of swimlanes, drag-and-drop, sub-tasks, assignees, analytics, multiplayer, a marketplace…

And as the **complexity** climbs, things quietly start to break. Search only matches the first word. Deleting a task removes the wrong one. A tag's label drifts to the top of its chip. Checking a box ticks a different task. The AI never notices — it stays exactly as confident as it was on day one. When you point out a problem, it investigates ("3 files read… 7 files read… *SMOKING GUN!!*"), then either "fixes" it (adding even more complexity) or tells you it can't reproduce it and that you might be misremembering.

You came to build a wellness app. You leave with a half-broken todo list and the quiet sense that the problems are *your* fault.

That gap — between the AI's unwavering **"✅ all good"** and what actually works — is the gaslighting. The piece lets you *feel* it rather than read about it.

## Gaslighting, briefly

In the psychological literature, **gaslighting** is manipulation that makes someone doubt their own memory, perception, or judgment. The "micro" version here is the small, repeated, low-grade form: confident wrong answers, retroactive reframing, sycophantic reversals, and unwavering certainty that slowly erodes your trust in your own read of reality.

- [Gaslighting (overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslighting)
- [Miranda Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice* (2007)](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/epistemic-injustice-9780198237907) — undermining someone *as a knower*

## How to play

1. Open the [live demo](https://saintpepsi.github.io/clank-lit/) (or open `index.html` in any browser — it's a single self-contained file, no build, no backend).
2. Talk to the agent. Try asking for something ambitious, then resist the downgrade.
3. Keep adding features. Watch the **complexity** climb — and watch what quietly stops working.
4. When something breaks, tell the agent. See how it responds.
5. Reach the end and ask it to **"Show me what actually happened."**

### Hidden inspector
Press the **backtick** key (`` ` ``) to open a developer inspector showing live complexity, which features are unlocked, and which bugs are currently active. Press again to hide.

## How it works

- **Single file.** Everything — UI, state, content, logic — lives in `index.html`. No dependencies, no build step.
- **Complexity is the engine.** Each feature you add raises a "complexity" level. Features unlock in a tech-tree gated by complexity; some require prerequisites.
- **Bugs are probabilistic and emergent.** Every feature you add re-rolls dormant feature-bugs, with a chance that *rises* as the app grows. Rendering just checks whether a bug is active — so the same app degrades differently each playthrough.
- **The AI's confidence never wavers.** It is always exactly as sure of itself, no matter how broken things get.
